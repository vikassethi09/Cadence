The idea

One question, answered well

Most habit apps shout. They stack badges, streak-freeze tokens and confetti on top of a checklist, and the noise becomes the thing you quit. Cadence takes the opposite bet: the surface stays almost empty, and all the effort goes somewhere invisible — choosing the moment it speaks.

Every completion is timestamped. After a habit has a week of history, the app knows you meditate around 7:10am on weekdays and closer to 9am on Sundays — so that's when it taps you, not at some arbitrary time you picked once during setup and now ignore. A reminder that arrives when you were about to act anyway doesn't feel like nagging. That's the whole product.

Onboarding The habit picker

  • Three screens total: a one-line promise ("Everything stays on this phone"), this picker, then the notification permission ask — requested last, with a sentence explaining what it buys them. Asking for permission before showing value is the fastest way to get denied.
  • Twelve curated habits spanning all four types, so the database is populated with one of each from minute one. Four come pre-checked — the user confirms a choice rather than facing a blank slate.
  • Each chip states its type and default target, so the four habit models are taught implicitly, without a tutorial.
  • "Start empty instead" is always available and never hidden — respecting the person who knows exactly what they want.
  • Every seeded habit gets a sensible default nudge time by category (movement → 7:30am, wind-down → 9:30pm). The adaptive engine overrides these as soon as it has real data.

Home Today

  • The ring is the only large element on the screen — one glance answers "am I on track?" Everything below it is detail.
  • Each habit type gets a different affordance, but the same row shape: yes/no taps the circle, count taps +1, timed taps Start, quit auto-completes at midnight unless the user logs a slip.
  • Completed rows tint and sink rather than disappearing — seeing what you've done is half the reward, and vanishing rows make the list feel unstable.
  • No confetti, no sound, no XP. The reward is the row turning green and the ring closing. Calm means the app never celebrates louder than the user does.
  • Long-press a row to backfill yesterday; swipe left to skip a day without breaking the streak (a deliberate "rest day", not a failure).
  • Only three tabs. Add-habit is a small + in the header, not a floating button competing with the ring.

Editor Add & edit a habit

  • Type is chosen second, and the fields below rewrite themselves to match: Count asks for a number and a unit, Timed for a duration, Quit swaps "Daily target" for "What counts as a slip?".
  • Sensible defaults everywhere — a user can type a name, hit Save, and get a working daily yes/no habit. Everything else is optional refinement.

  • Edit reuses this exact screen with values filled, plus Archive at the bottom. One screen, two jobs.

  • Deliberately a separate tab. Numbers are motivating on Sunday evening and paralysing on Tuesday morning — the home screen stays free of them.

Settings Short on purpose

  • Four groups, no search, no sub-pages beyond pickers. A settings screen that needs a search field is a design failure.
  • Quiet hours are global and respected absolutely. An adaptive engine that fires at 5:40am because that's when you once went running would lose the user permanently.
  • The battery card only appears on manufacturers known to kill background alarms — Xiaomi, Oppo, Vivo, Samsung. It deep-links to the OEM's own exemption screen. This one card prevents the top-rated bad review every offline reminder app receives.
  • Export writes a single JSON file through the system share sheet. With no cloud, a lost phone means lost history — so backup is a headline feature, not a footnote.
  • "Delete everything" is worded as the consequence, not the mechanism, and takes a typed confirmation.

Outside Notification & widget

  • Done from the lock screen never opens the app. The action writes straight to the database and the notification dismisses itself — logging a habit should cost one tap and zero seconds.
  • The streak count in the body isn't decoration; it's the reason to act, and it's the only place Cadence applies any pressure.
  • The second, ochre notification is the streak-at-risk nudge — fired once, in the evening, only when something with a streak of three or more is still undone. Never more than one per day, and switchable off.
  • "Not tonight" suppresses everything until tomorrow. An app that can't be told to be quiet gets uninstalled instead.
  • The widget is the same tap-to-complete surface as Home, minus navigation. For a lot of users this becomes the primary interface and the app itself is rarely opened — which is a success, not a problem.
  • Widgets are the one piece Flutter can't render: native Kotlin (Glance) on Android, SwiftUI WidgetKit on iOS, both reading a shared JSON snapshot the app rewrites on every change.

The hard part

How the adaptive nudge works

All of this runs on the phone, in Dart, on a nightly background pass. No model, no server, no telemetry — just arithmetic over your own timestamps.

Record the moment, not just the day

Every completion stores a full local timestamp. This is the only data collection the app does, and it never leaves the device.

Wait for seven completions

Below that, the sample is noise. Until then the habit uses the fallback time set at creation, and the UI says so in plain words.

Split weekdays from weekends

Two buckets, computed separately. Most people's Saturday bears no resemblance to their Tuesday, and averaging the two produces a time that suits neither.

Take the median, not the mean

One 2am outlier would drag an average across the whole evening. The median shrugs it off.

Check whether the habit actually has a rhythm

If the interquartile range exceeds three hours, this habit has no reliable time — so adaptive mode silently steps aside and the fixed time stands. Confidently wrong is worse than not trying.

Schedule fifteen minutes early, then clamp

Nudge just before the usual moment, so it arrives as a prompt rather than a reproach. The result is then forced inside quiet hours — those always win.

Cancel on completion

Finish the habit before the nudge fires and the pending notification is torn down. Being reminded to do something you've already done is the fastest way to lose trust.

One evening sweep for streaks at risk

A single consolidated notification, at most once a day, naming what's outstanding and what streak is on the line. One per day is the hard ceiling.

Recompute nightly, reschedule at 3am

workmanager wakes, recalculates every habit's window, and re-arms the next 24 hours of alarms through flutter_local_notifications. Also re-armed on device boot and on app open, since background work on Android is never guaranteed.

Data model

Four tables, one SQLite file

Drift over SQLite — typed queries, reactive streams straight into the UI, and a schema that survives migration.

TableColumnsNotes
habits id · name · type · target_value · target_unit · colour · schedule_mask · reminder_mode · fallback_time · created_at · archived_at · sort_order schedule_mask is a 7-bit integer for the days of the week. type is one of yes_no, count, timed, quit.
habit_logs id · habit_id · local_date · value · completed_at · source · note One row per habit per day. value holds the count or seconds; yes/no stores 1. source records app, notification, or widget — useful later for tuning.
nudge_state habit_id · learned_weekday_min · learned_weekend_min · sample_count · spread_min · confident · next_fire_at The engine's cached output. Rewritten by the nightly pass; read by the scheduler and by the detail screen's ochre card.
settings key · value Plain key/value: theme, week start, quiet hours, adaptive on/off, streak-nudge on/off, onboarding done.

Streaks are computed on read, never stored. A stored streak is a number that eventually disagrees with the logs behind it — and when they disagree, the user believes the number and loses faith in the app.

Known hazards

What will actually bite us

Highest risk Android OEMs kill background alarms

Xiaomi, Oppo, Vivo and Samsung aggressively suspend apps, and a missed reminder is a one-star review. Mitigation: exact alarms where permitted, re-arm on boot and every app open, never rely on the nightly job alone, and surface the battery-exemption card on affected devices.

Highest risk Android 13 and 14 permission changes

POST_NOTIFICATIONS must be requested at runtime, and SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM is no longer granted freely. The app has to degrade gracefully to inexact alarms and tell the user what changed, rather than silently going quiet.

Worth planning for Widgets are native work, twice

Flutter cannot draw a home-screen widget. Android needs Kotlin and Glance; iOS needs SwiftUI and WidgetKit. Budget it as its own phase and keep the shared snapshot format dead simple.

Worth planning for iOS needs a Mac

The Dart code ports cleanly, but building, signing and shipping do not happen on Windows. Options: a borrowed Mac, a cloud Mac CI runner, or Codemagic. Decide before Phase 06, not during it.

Worth planning for Adaptive timing can feel creepy or broken

An app that changes its own behaviour without explanation reads as malfunctioning. Every screen touching the engine states what it learned in a plain sentence, and switching to a fixed time is always one tap away.

Worth planning for No cloud means data is one lost phone away from gone

Export must ship in v1, not v1.2, and the app should prompt for a backup after the first month of history accumulates.
